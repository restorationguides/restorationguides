Fire & Smoke Restoration

Corrosion control, soot profile identification, and structural deodorization protocols for we professionals.

Dry Soot (Natural)

Derived from wood, paper, or natural fibers. High-heat fires. It is powdery and lacks oils, making it easier to remove via dry-cleaning methods.

Dry Removal Only

Wet Soot (Synthetic)

Derived from plastics, rubber, and polymers. Low-heat, smoldering fires. Oily, smeary, and high-odor. Requires chemical bond-breaking.

Alkaline Degreasing

Protein Soot

Derived from kitchen fires (fats/oils). Virtually invisible but causes extreme pungent odors and permanent discoloration to finishes.

Enzyme Treatment

Fuel Oil (Puff-back)

Result of furnace or boiler malfunction. Distributes heavy soot through HVAC systems. Requires specialized mechanical remediation.

HVAC Replacement

Restorative Cleaning Protocols

Initial Corrosion Control

We prioritize metal surfaces immediately to prevent permanent pitting from acidic soot residues.

Structural Deodorization

Multi-step process: Debris removal, thermal fogging (penetration), and Ozone/Hydroxyl (atmospheric neutralization).

HVAC Integrity

Flex duct and fiberboard must be replaced. Metal ductwork requires camera-verified mechanical cleaning.

The pH Factor

Understanding chemical bonds is critical for soot removal:

  • Alkaline (pH 8-14): Required for wet/synthetic soot and protein-based residues.
  • Acidic (pH 0-6): Used for neutralizing alkaline salts or removing mineralized fire residues.
  • Dry Sponges: Used exclusively for Natural (Dry) soot to prevent pushing particles deeper into porous surfaces.
